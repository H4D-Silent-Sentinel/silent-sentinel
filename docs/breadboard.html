Silent Sentinel: demo node on the breadboard

Turn the breadboard so row 1 is at the top and column a is on the left. Every jumper below goes from one hole to another, named by column letter and row number (for example a43). Holes in the same row on the same side of the centre channel (a–e, or f–j) are connected inside the board.

Today's demo build: ESP32-S3, one ICS-43434 mic, one RFM95W radio. Nothing uses the + / − rails, and the KB2040 is not part of this build.

Wire from column h. Jumper heads are too thick to sit right beside the parts, and every hole f–i in a row is connected, so h works the same as i.

1Place the parts

  1. ESP32-S3: USB ports toward row 63, left header in column b, rows 42–63: b42 = 3V3 … b63 = GND (the board itself spans b–j, rows 42–64). Every ESP32 wire plugs into the free hole beside it in column a.
  2. Mic: 6 pins in column j, rows 1–6, SEL at j1, 3V at j6. Board hangs off the right edge over the rails.
  3. RFM95W: 9-pin edge in column j, rows 21–29, VIN at j21, RST at j29. Module hangs off the right edge. 78 mm antenna wire on ANT. EN (row 23) stays unconnected.

4Check it

  1. Plug the USB cable into the same port as before. The LED turns blue (listening).
  2. Tell Claude; it turns on the live readout. Whistle or play the demo drone sound near the mic: the level jumps, and the drone sound turns the LED red within about a second.
