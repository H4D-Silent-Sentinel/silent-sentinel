Silent Sentinel: first node on the breadboard

Turn the breadboard so row 1 is at the top and column a is on the left. Every jumper below goes from one hole to another, named by column letter and row number (for example a43). Holes in the same row on the same side of the centre channel (a–e, or f–j) are connected inside the board.

Unplug the USB cable while you wire. Everything runs on 3.3 V from the ESP32; nothing goes to the 5V pin.

Check the mic pin order. The drawing assumes the mic's six pins read 3V, GND, BCLK, DOUT, LRCL, SEL from top to bottom. If yours are in a different order, keep the rows the same and wire by the labels printed on the mic board.

0Place the parts

  1. ESP32-S3: USB ports toward row 63. Its left header (J1, labelled 3V3, 3V3, RST, 4, 5, 6 …) goes in column b, rows 40–61, so b40 is 3V3 and b61 is GND. Column a stays free: that's where every wire to the ESP32 plugs in.
  2. Mic M0: its 6 pins in column a, rows 4–9, board hanging out to the left over the rails, sound hole facing up.
  3. Mic M1: its 6 pins in column j, rows 4–9, hanging out to the right.
  4. RFM95W (optional today): its 9-pin edge in column j, rows 21–29 (VIN at row 21), module hanging out to the right. Only that edge needs a header.
  5. If the red/blue rail lines have a break halfway down the board, bridge each rail across the break with a short jumper.

4Check it

  1. Plug the USB cable into the same port as before. The LED turns blue (listening).
  2. Tell Claude; it turns on the live readout. Whistle or play the demo drone sound near the mics: the level jumps, and the drone sound turns the LED red within about a second.
